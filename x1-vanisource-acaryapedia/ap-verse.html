Upadeśāmṛta 1

The marks of a tridaṇḍi-gosvāmī

← All eleven textsText 2 →

Text

vāco vegaṁ manasaḥ krodha-vegaṁjihvā-vegam udaropastha-vegametān vegān yo viṣaheta dhīraḥsarvām apīmāṁ pṛthivīṁ sa śiṣyāt

Word for word machine translation, draft

yaḥ — who; dhīraḥ — the sober, intelligent person; vācaḥ vegam — the urge of speech; manasaḥ [vegam] — the urge of the mind; krodha-vegam — the urge of anger; jihvā-vegam — the urge of the tongue; udara-upastha-vegam — the urge of the belly and the genitals; etān vegān — these urges; viṣaheta — is able to bear; saḥ — he; imām sarvām pṛthivīm api — even this whole earth; śiṣyāt — can govern.

Translation machine translation, draft

The sober person who can bear these six urges, the urge of speech, of the mind, of anger, of the tongue, of the belly and of the genitals, can govern even this whole earth.

BhāṣāBhaktivinoda Ṭhākura (Bengali verse)

The urge of speech, the urge of the mind, the urge of anger, the urge of the tongue and the lowly urges of the belly and the genitals: one who bears these six urges, sheltered in Kṛṣṇa's name, can rule the world, having conquered fear. Surrender alone is full of devotion to Kṛṣṇa, and giving up what is unfavourable to bhakti is its limb. Bearing the six urges, sheltered in yukta-vairāgya, one becomes free of offences to the name and fearless.

PadyānuvādaBhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī (Bengali verse)

Talk of anything other than Kṛṣṇa is the urge of speech. When lust is not fulfilled, anger rises in the mind. One who lives for tasty food is a servant of the tongue; one who eats too much rests his hopes in the belly. The servant of women, the slave of lust, is driven by the urge of the genitals. One who keeps these six urges always under control is a gosvāmī and conquers the world.
